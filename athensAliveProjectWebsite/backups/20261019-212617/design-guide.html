Give the logo room

Suggested clear space: leave at least the height of the “A” in the wordmark around all sides. Keep text, borders, and other graphics outside that area.

Suggested minimum widths: long logo 240 px on screen / 60 mm in print; short logo 160 px / 40 mm. Check the landmark details at final size and increase the mark if they become unclear.

Keep the artwork intact

Preserve the original proportions. Use the approved black or beige artwork with a contrasting background. Avoid stretching, rotating, recoloring individual elements, adding effects, or placing the logo over busy photographs.

Keep the lettering as supplied artwork; do not recreate it with the body typeface.

02 / Palette

Color with a purpose.

Beige creates a warm foundation. Blue organizes the hierarchy, orange highlights actions, and green adds energy through lines and supporting accents. These roles follow the supplied brand board; hex values come from the current project stylesheet.

Keep text readable

Use dark ink (#2b2b2b) for paragraphs on beige. Keep orange and green primarily in shapes, icons, buttons, and decorative accents; use dark text for small labels. A green underline can support a subheading while its words remain dark. Use dark labels on orange buttons and a visible keyboard focus outline.

For print, give the printer the RGB / hex palette and request a color proof. CMYK output depends on the printer and paper profile.

04 / Application

Put the pieces together.

Buttons and accents

Use orange for primary actions, a dark readable label, rounded corners, and generous spacing. Green lines provide a supporting accent.

Find visual assets

Page rhythm

Start with beige, introduce blue section headings, and use a restrained brand-color gradient in hero areas. Give logos and content room to breathe. Use a consistent spacing rhythm of 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, and 64 px.
